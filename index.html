The map baseline follows the historical Gleason disk layout: the North Pole is at the centre, longitude meridians are straight radial rays, and equal latitude increments are placed at equal radial intervals. The drawing convention assigns 60 nautical miles (111.12 km) to each degree of latitude, giving a 20,001.6 km outer-disk radius in the planar coordinate model. This is a historical planar construction, not independent proof of real-world distances or shapes; validation against external observations is kept separate from the drawing geometry. The A–B readout, scale bar and polygon perimeter use ordinary Euclidean lengths in the displayed map plane; polygon area uses the planar shoelace formula. Country, coastline, airport and capital coordinates are geographic reference data for rendering and search.
