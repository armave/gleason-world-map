WGS84 radial calibration

Dₙ(φ) = meridional distance south from 90°N
r = Dₙ(φ)
x = r · cos(λ)
y = −r · sin(λ)
WGS84 disk radius at 90°S = 20,003.93 km
LatitudeWGS84 distance from 90°NDisk radius

The radial distance is fixed to the WGS84 ellipsoidal meridian distance from the North Pole; there is no adjustable exponent. Thus distances from the map centre are true along every meridian. Distances between arbitrary off-centre places are calculated separately as WGS84 geodesics. East longitudes run counter-clockwise, matching the Gleason sheet. Local scale and area distortion vary across this flat projection.

Radial scale is 1.000×. East–west map stretch compares disk scale with the WGS84 ellipsoid; multiply a flat east–west distance by its decoder (the reciprocal) for local ground distance. Map-area stretch is the product of radial and tangential scales. Click a location to inspect local distortion.

Country outlines use Natural Earth; capitals use country-data; U.S. county-equivalent label points use the 2026 U.S. Census Gazetteer (public domain). Ground distances and areas use the WGS84 reference ellipsoid. The radial scale is true from the North Pole along meridians; arbitrary point-to-point distances are shortest WGS84 geodesics, and polygon areas use ellipsoidal polygon area. The scale bar is a local ground scale near the current view centre and changes with pan and zoom. The map is a flat projection, not a flat-earth physical model; local distortion is shown explicitly.
