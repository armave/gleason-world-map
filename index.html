Country boundaries and coastlines use Natural Earth; capitals use country-data; U.S. county-equivalent search points use the 2026 U.S. Census Gazetteer (public domain); the Antarctic boundary is rendered on the Southern Disk as the continuous outer Ice Wall rim. The map uses two equal-radius 2D circular disks of radius 10,000.8 km hinged at the Equator. Northern and Southern coordinates share one projection function. Same-disk distances are Euclidean planar chords; cross-Equator distances are the sum of two planar chords through the optimized matching Equator-rim longitude. Polygon boundaries and coastlines are clipped at latitude 0° before projection. These are distances and areas in the requested planar model and are not independently validated ground-distance claims. Historical reference: Alexander Gleason, “New Standard Map of the World” (1892), Boston Public Library / Digital Commonwealth.
