Flat-disk radial calibration

R = fitted ADS-B flight-mileage scale
φ = latitude · π / 180
θ = π/2 − φ
t = θ / π
q = ((t + ε)^p − ε^p) / ((1 + ε)^p − ε^p)
p = 0.9 · ε = 0.005
r = πR · q
x = r · cos(λ)
y = −r · sin(λ)
Flat-disk radius = πR

The radial scale R is generated from median observed airport-pair flight mileage, estimated by integrating ADS-B ground speed over recorded time. It is not anchored to a published shortest-distance table. The flight-empirical estimate is typical observed flown mileage, not a shortest point-to-point path.

The disk uses a smooth, monotonic radial warp of latitude-ring spacing. It preserves the North Pole at the centre and 90°S at the circular outer rim. The A–B connector is one straight projected segment; its primary label is the ADS-B flight-mileage estimate, with the raw disk chord shown as a secondary reference. The estimate is fitted from ground-speed/time observations, with polar extrapolation status and nearest supporting routes available in the measurement details. The 60°S Restricted Line is a visual reference only; direct connectors and area polygons may cross it.

The map is displayed as a flat Gleason-style disk. A–B uses one straight projected segment and displays the ADS-B empirical mileage estimate as primary, with the raw Euclidean chord as a secondary reference. The estimate is not a shortest path or guaranteed flight track. Calibration support and nearest reference routes are available in the measurement details; polar extrapolation is identified there without a warning panel. The 60°S Restricted Line does not alter the estimate. Polygon area and perimeter remain planar.
