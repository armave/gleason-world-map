Flat-disk radial calibration

R = 6,371 km (radial scale)
φ = latitude · π / 180
θ = π/2 − φ
r = R · θ
x = r · cos(λ)
y = −r · sin(λ)
Flat-disk radius = πR = 20,015.09 km

The disk coordinates are positioned using r = Rθ. A–B distances use the shortest WGS84 surface geodesic at every latitude. The 60°S Restricted Line is a visual reference only and does not change the distance calculation; direct connectors and area polygons may cross it. East longitudes run counter-clockwise, matching the Gleason sheet.

The map is displayed as a flat Gleason-style disk. A–B distance always uses the shortest WGS84 ellipsoidal surface geodesic, even when an endpoint or the shortest route lies south of 60°S. The 60°S Restricted Line is a visual reference, not a metric switch or a barrier. The dashed A–B connector remains a straight projected map segment; polygon area and perimeter remain planar.
